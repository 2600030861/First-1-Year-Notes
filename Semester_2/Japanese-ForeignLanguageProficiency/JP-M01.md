# Japanese (Foreign Language Proficiency) – Module 1

## Kanji & Vocabulary

*Friend-style notes: intuition first, then the formula, then a small example.*

## The big picture

Japanese is written with **three scripts at the same time**, and that is the first thing nobody tells you clearly.

- **漢字（かんじ, kanji）** – characters borrowed from Chinese. Each one carries a *meaning* and one or more *sounds*.
- **ひらがな（hiragana）** – the round, curvy letters. They spell grammar glue: verb endings, particles, and native words.
- **カタカナ（katakana）** – the same sounds as hiragana but in angular shapes. Mostly for foreign words: パン（pan, bread）, コーヒー（koohii, coffee）.

The analogy that makes it click: **kanji are photographs, kana are the alphabet.** A photograph (山) shows you the thing instantly, but you still need letters to spell out how the sentence around it works. That is why a Japanese sentence is a mixed picture-and-letters strip: わたしは 山に 行きます（watashi wa yama ni ikimasu, "I go to the mountain"）.

Why bother with kanji at N5? Because about **80 to 100 characters** unlock the majority of what you actually meet — station signs, prices, menus, dates on a form, your own name. Japanese schools teach roughly 80 characters in first grade, and the JLPT N5 list sits in that same neighbourhood. These notes give you **exactly 80**, grouped so they stick.

## Basics you must know first

**1. Hiragana — the 46 basic sounds.** You do not need to write them beautifully yet, but you must be able to *read* them, because every reading in the kanji tables below is written in hiragana.

|  | a | i | u | e | o |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **–** | あ | い | う | え | お |
| **k** | か | き | く | け | こ |
| **s** | さ | し | す | せ | そ |
| **t** | た | ち | つ | て | と |
| **n** | な | に | ぬ | ね | の |
| **h** | は | ひ | ふ | へ | ほ |
| **m** | ま | み | む | め | も |
| **y** | や | (い) | ゆ | (え) | よ |
| **r** | ら | り | る | れ | ろ |
| **w** | わ | – | – | – | を |
| **n** | ん | | | | |

Two sounds to fix in your head: し is **shi** (not "si"), ち is **chi**, つ is **tsu**, ふ is **fu**.

**2. Katakana** is the same 46 sounds in different clothes: ア イ ウ エ オ, カ キ ク ケ コ … You will meet it in loanwords (パン, バス, テレビ). Learn to *recognise* it first; writing can come later.

**3. Romaji system used in these notes.** I use Hepburn-style romaji with long vowels written as double letters: とうきょう → **Toukyou**, がっこう → **gakkou**, おはよう → **ohayou**. The small っ doubles the next consonant (がっこう = ga + k + kou). The particle は is read **wa**, and the particle へ is read **e** — that is not a typo, it is a spelling convention you simply memorise.

**4. A kanji has two reading families.**
- **On-yomi（音読み）** – the Chinese-style reading. Shown in **katakana** in dictionaries. Usually appears when two or more kanji sit together: 日本（にほん, Nihon, Japan）.
- **Kun-yomi（訓読み）** – the native Japanese reading. Shown in **hiragana**. Usually appears when the kanji stands alone or with a hiragana tail: 日（ひ, hi, day）.

**5. Strokes are counted and ordered.** Every kanji has a fixed stroke count and a fixed order. Nobody fails an exam for messy handwriting, but wrong order makes characters unrecognisable and slows you down badly later. Learn the order from day one.

## 1.1 Kanji

### Intuition

Think of each kanji as a **tiny photograph of an idea**, and the readings as the *name you call that photograph* depending on who is talking. 山 is a mountain: three peaks, one tall in the middle. 川 is a river: three lines flowing down. 口 is a mouth: an open square. 目 is an eye: a box with two lines inside, stood on its side. 木 is a tree: a trunk, two branches, two roots. 雨 is rain: drops falling inside a window frame. 魚 is a fish standing on its tail.

Once you see the photo, the character stops being a random scribble. That is the whole trick, and it is why these notes always give you the picture first.

### The idea, step by step

**Step 1 — decide which reading you are looking at.** Look at what surrounds the kanji.

- Kanji + kanji (no hiragana between) → usually **on-yomi**: 学生（がくせい, gakusei, student）.
- Kanji alone, or kanji + hiragana tail → usually **kun-yomi**: 食べる（たべる, taberu, to eat）.

**Step 2 — spot the radical（部首, bushu）.** A radical is the repeated component that hints at meaning. The same radical shows up in dozens of characters, so learning it multiplies your guessing power.

- 言（ごんべん, gonben, "speech"）→ 語（language）, 読（read）
- 日（ひへん, "sun"）→ 時（time）, 日, 月? no — 月 is its own radical
- 木（きへん, "tree"）→ 校（school）
- 囗（くにがまえ, "enclosure"）→ 国（country）
- 艹（くさかんむり, "grass"）→ 花（flower）
- 穴（あなかんむり, "hole"）→ 空（sky, empty）
- 宀（うかんむり, "roof"）→ 字（character）

**Step 3 — follow the stroke-order rules.** Five rules cover almost everything at N5:

1. **Top to bottom** — 三: the top line first, then middle, then bottom.
2. **Left to right** — 川: left line, middle line, right line.
3. **Horizontal before vertical** where they cross — 十: the horizontal 一 first, then the vertical 丨.
4. **Outside before inside, then close the box** — 国: the frame first, then 玉 inside, then the bottom line closes it.
5. **Dots and small final strokes go last** — 犬: the dog body first, the little dot on top-right last.

**Step 4 — read the table entry in this order:** character → on-yomi (katakana) → kun-yomi (hiragana) → English meaning → a word you can actually use. The example word is the important column. A reading you never use is a reading you will forget. One honest caveat: these lists give the one or two readings you actually need at N5, not every reading a dictionary lists — 生 and 来 have more readings than shown, and I have deliberately left out the rare ones rather than guess at how they are taught in your class.

**Example question:** Look at these three words and say whether the 日 in each is being read with its on-yomi or its kun-yomi: 日本（にほん）, 日曜日（にちようび）, 日（ひ）.

**Solution:**

- Step 1: 日本 is kanji + kanji with no hiragana between them → compound → on-yomi. The reading is に, a shortened form of the on-yomi ニチ. So: **on-yomi**.
- Step 2: 日曜日 is a three-kanji compound, all kanji, no hiragana glue → on-yomi readings: ニチ + ヨウ + ビ. So: **on-yomi**.
- Step 3: 日 stands completely alone, with nothing attached → native word → kun-yomi ひ. So: **kun-yomi**.

**Final answer:** 日本 = on-yomi (に), 日曜日 = on-yomi (にち), 日 alone = kun-yomi (ひ).

⚠️ **Common trap:** the "kanji + kanji means on-yomi" rule is a strong hint, not a law. 日本 is a genuine exception-flavoured case (に is an old on-yomi), and 今日（きょう, kyou, today）is read in a way that follows neither reading neatly. Treat the rule as 90 percent reliable and learn the odd ones as whole words.

### Theme 1 — Numbers 1 to 10

| 漢字 | On-yomi (katakana) | Kun-yomi (hiragana) | Meaning | Example word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 一 | イチ、イツ | ひと- | one | 一つ（ひとつ, hitotsu）= one thing |
| 二 | ニ | ふた- | two | 二つ（ふたつ, futatsu）= two things |
| 三 | サン | みっ-、み- | three | 三つ（みっつ, mittsu）= three things |
| 四 | シ | よん、よっ- | four | 四つ（よっつ, yottsu）= four things |
| 五 | ゴ | いつ- | five | 五つ（いつつ, itsutsu）= five things |
| 六 | ロク | むっ-、む- | six | 六つ（むっつ, muttsu）= six things |
| 七 | シチ | なな、なな- | seven | 七つ（ななつ, nanatsu）= seven things |
| 八 | ハチ | やっ-、や- | eight | 八つ（やっつ, yattsu）= eight things |
| 九 | キュウ、ク | ここの- | nine | 九つ（ここのつ, kokonotsu）= nine things |
| 十 | ジュウ | とお、と- | ten | 十日（とおか, tooka）= the 10th of the month |

**Stroke counts:** 一 1, 二 2, 三 3, 四 5, 五 4, 六 4, 七 2, 八 2, 九 2, 十 2. **Radical idea:** 一 is itself a radical ("one"). 十 is a crossing of 一 and 丨 and appears inside 千 and 半.

**Memory hooks:** 三 is literally three lines. 五 has a "folded" shape like a hand counting five. 七 looks like a 7 rotated. 八 opens outward like the two legs of the character for "eight" in a race.

**Example question:** Read these aloud: 三百円, 六千, 八つ, 十.

**Solution:**

- 三百円 → 百 takes the on-yomi ヒャク, but after 三 it voices to **びゃく** (this voicing is called *rendaku*, "sequential voicing"). 円 is エン. So さんびゃくえん（sanbyaku en, 300 yen）.
- 六千 → 六 = ロク, 千 = セン. No voicing here. ろくせん（rokusen, 6,000）.
- 八つ → the counter つ needs the kun-yomi やっ-. やっつ（yattsu, eight things）.
- 十 → on-yomi ジュウ, read じゅう（juu, ten）.

**Final answer:** さんびゃくえん / ろくせん / やっつ / じゅう.

### Theme 2 — Big numbers, money and half

| 漢字 | On-yomi | Kun-yomi | Meaning | Example word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 百 | ヒャク | – | hundred | 六百（ろっぴゃく, roppyaku）= 600 |
| 千 | セン | ち | thousand | 三千（さんぜん, sanzen）= 3,000 |
| 万 | マン、バン | – | ten thousand | 一万円（いちまんえん, ichiman en）= 10,000 yen |
| 円 | エン | まる- | yen; circle | 百円（ひゃくえん, hyaku en）= 100 yen |
| 半 | ハン | なかば | half | 半分（はんぶん, hanbun）= half |

**Stroke counts:** 百 6, 千 3, 万 3, 円 4, 半 5. **Radical idea:** 百 is 一 sitting on top of 白（しろ, white）. 千 is 十 with a small slant on top. 円 is an enclosure radical 冂 with strokes inside. 半 is a "ten" (十) with two dots above it, which is exactly how you should remember it.

**Number-building rule:** Japanese groups numbers by **four zeros**, not three. `一万 = 10,000`, `十万 = 100,000`, `百万 = 1,000,000`. So 12,345 is 一万二千三百四十五（いちまんにせんさんびゃくよんじゅうご）.

**Example question:** Write and read 25,000 yen in Japanese.

**Solution:**

- Step 1: 25,000 = 2 × 10,000 + 5 × 1,000 → 二万五千.
- Step 2: readings — 二 = ニ, 万 = マン → にまん; 五 = ゴ, 千 = セン → ごせん.
- Step 3: add the currency word 円 = エン.

**Final answer:** 二万五千円（にまんごせんえん, niman gosen en）.

⚠️ **Common trap:** 三百 is さんびゃく, 六百 is ろっぴゃく, 八百 is はっぴゃく — the 百 changes sound. Similarly 三千 is さんぜん. Learn these three or four voicing patterns as sounds, not as theory.

### Theme 3 — Days and time

| 漢字 | On-yomi | Kun-yomi | Meaning | Example word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 日 | ニチ、ジツ | ひ、か | day; sun | 毎日（まいにち, mainichi）= every day |
| 月 | ゲツ、ガツ | つき | month; moon | 月曜日（げつようび, getsuyoubi）= Monday |
| 火 | カ | ひ | fire | 火曜日（かようび, kayoubi）= Tuesday |
| 水 | スイ | みず | water | 水（みず, mizu）= water |
| 木 | モク、ボク | き | tree; wood | 木曜日（もくようび, mokuyoubi）= Thursday |
| 金 | キン、コン | かね | gold; money | お金（おかね, okane）= money |
| 土 | ド、ト | つち | earth; soil | 土曜日（どようび, doyoubi）= Saturday |
| 年 | ネン | とし | year | 今年（ことし, kotoshi）= this year |
| 時 | ジ | とき | hour; time | 三時（さんじ, sanji）= three o'clock |
| 分 | フン、ブン | わ- | minute; part | 五分（ごふん, gofun）= five minutes |
| 今 | コン、キン | いま | now | 今日（きょう, kyou）= today |
| 午 | ゴ | – | noon | 午前（ごぜん, gozen）= a.m. |

**Stroke counts:** 日 4, 月 4, 火 4, 水 4, 木 4, 金 8, 土 3, 年 6, 時 10, 分 4, 今 4, 午 4. **Radical idea:** 時 = 日 (sun) + 寺, because time is measured by the sun. 明（あか.るい, bright）also uses 日 — a sun plus a moon is brightness. 分 has 八 on top of 刀 ("a blade dividing something into parts"), which is why it means both "minute" (a division of the hour) and "to divide/understand" (分かる, wakaru).

**The weekday pattern:** 月 火 水 木 金 土 日 = moon, fire, water, tree, gold, earth, sun. That is the order, Monday to Sunday, and Japanese children learn it as a chant. Sunday (日曜日) is the sun, which is why it sits at the end.

**Example question:** Read 午後三時半 and say what it means.

**Solution:**

- 午後 = ご + ご → ごご（gogo, afternoon / p.m.）.
- 三 = さん, 時 = じ → さんじ（sanji, three o'clock）.
- 半 = はん（han, half）→ "half past".
- Assemble in the Japanese order: p.m. + hour + half.

**Final answer:** ごごさんじはん（gogo sanji han）= 3:30 p.m.

### Theme 4 — Family and people

| 漢字 | On-yomi | Kun-yomi | Meaning | Example word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 人 | ジン、ニン | ひと | person | 日本人（にほんじん, nihonjin）= Japanese person |
| 父 | フ | ちち | father | お父さん（おとうさん, otousan）= (someone's) father |
| 母 | ボ | はは | mother | お母さん（おかあさん, okaasan）= (someone's) mother |
| 子 | シ | こ | child | 子ども（こども, kodomo）= child |
| 女 | ジョ、ニョ | おんな | woman | 女の人（おんなのひと, onna no hito）= woman |
| 男 | ダン、ナン | おとこ | man | 男の子（おとこのこ, otokonoko）= boy |
| 友 | ユウ | とも | friend | 友だち（ともだち, tomodachi）= friend |
| 名 | メイ、ミョウ | な | name | 名前（なまえ, namae）= name |
| 兄 | ケイ、キョウ | あに | older brother | お兄さん（おにいさん, oniisan）= (someone's) older brother |

**Stroke counts:** 人 2, 父 4, 母 5, 子 3, 女 3, 男 7, 友 4, 名 6, 兄 5. **Radical idea:** 人 is a person standing with two legs — and it flattens into 亻 on the left side of characters (as in 休, "a person resting against a tree"). 女 and 子 are both radicals in their own right; 好（す.き, to like）is literally 女 + 子. 男 is 田 (rice field) + 力 (power): the person who works the field with strength.

**The 父 / お父さん split:** 父（ちち）and 母（はは）are for *your own* parents when talking to others. お父さん / お母さん are for *someone else's* parents, and also how you address your own parents at home. Mixing these up is the classic beginner slip.

**Example question:** Read 男の子 and 女の人, and explain why の appears.

**Solution:**

- 男 = おとこ (kun-yomi, standing alone) → 男の子 = おとこのこ.
- 女 = おんな, 人 = ひと → 女の人 = おんなのひと.
- The の is the linking particle: "man's child" = boy, "woman's person" = woman. It works like an apostrophe-s in English, but broader.

**Final answer:** おとこのこ (boy) / おんなのひと (woman); の links two nouns where the first describes or owns the second.

### Theme 5 — Nature and animals

| 漢字 | On-yomi | Kun-yomi | Meaning | Example word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 山 | サン | やま | mountain | 富士山（ふじさん, Fujisan）= Mount Fuji |
| 川 | セン | かわ | river | 川（かわ, kawa）= river |
| 天 | テン | あめ、あま | heaven; sky | 天気（てんき, tenki）= weather |
| 気 | キ、ケ | – | spirit; mood | 元気（げんき, genki）= healthy, lively |
| 雨 | ウ | あめ | rain | 大雨（おおあめ, ooame）= heavy rain |
| 空 | クウ | そら、あ-、から | sky; empty | 空気（くうき, kuuki）= air |
| 花 | カ | はな | flower | 花見（はなみ, hanami）= cherry-blossom viewing |
| 犬 | ケン | いぬ | dog | 子犬（こいぬ, koinu）= puppy |
| 魚 | ギョ | さかな | fish | 金魚（きんぎょ, kingyo）= goldfish |

**Stroke counts:** 山 3, 川 3, 天 4, 気 6, 雨 8, 空 8, 花 7, 犬 4, 魚 11. **Radical idea:** 天 is 大 (big) with a line above it — "the biggest thing above you" is the sky. 空 has the 穴 (hole) radical on top of 工: a hollow space. 花 carries the grass radical 艹 because plants grow from the ground. 魚 is drawn standing upright; its four dots at the bottom are the fins.

**Note on 気:** 気 is a *suffix-like* character that shows up in mood and energy words — 元気（げんき, healthy）, 天気（てんき, weather）, 電気（でんき, electricity）. Learn it as "energy/air/mood" rather than as one word.

**Example question:** What do 天気, 大雨 and 花火 mean, and how is 火 being read in 花火?

**Solution:**

- 天気 = テン + キ → てんき (weather).
- 大雨 = 大 (おお, big) + 雨 (あめ, rain) → おおあめ (heavy rain). Note 雨 keeps its kun-yomi because 大 here is a prefix-like "big".
- 花火 = 花 (はな, flower) + 火 (ひ, fire) → はなび, literally "fire flowers" = fireworks. 火 reads ひ (kun-yomi), but voices to び because it sits second in the compound.

**Final answer:** weather / heavy rain / fireworks (はなび).

### Theme 6 — Body parts

| 漢字 | On-yomi | Kun-yomi | Meaning | Example word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 口 | コウ、ク | くち | mouth | 入口（いりぐち, iriguchi）= entrance |
| 目 | モク | め | eye | 目（め, me）= eye |
| 耳 | ジ | みみ | ear | 耳（みみ, mimi）= ear |
| 手 | シュ | て | hand | 手紙（てがみ, tegami）= letter |
| 足 | ソク | あし | foot; leg | 足（あし, ashi）= foot, leg |

**Stroke counts:** 口 3, 目 5, 耳 6, 手 4, 足 7. **Radical idea:** 口 is the mouth radical and appears in 言-adjacent characters and in 入口 / 出口. 目 is an eye turned 90 degrees — the outer box is the eyelid, the two inner lines the pupil. 手 is a hand with fingers; on the left of characters it becomes 扌. 足 is a leg with a foot below it.

**Handy compounds:** 入口（いりぐち, entrance）, 出口（でぐち, exit）, 手紙（てがみ, letter — literally "paper hand"), 手足（てあし, arms and legs）.

**Example question:** A station sign says 出口. Which way do you walk, and how is it read?

**Solution:**

- 出 = "to come out" (not in our 80, but recognisable), 口 = くち (mouth/opening).
- Compound reading: で + ぐち → でぐち, with 口 voicing to ぐち.
- Meaning: "the mouth you go out of" = **exit**.

**Final answer:** でぐち（deguchi）— exit. The opposite sign 入口 is いりぐち（iriguchi）, entrance.

### Theme 7 — Directions and position

| 漢字 | On-yomi | Kun-yomi | Meaning | Example word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 上 | ジョウ | うえ、あ-、のぼ- | up; above | 上手（じょうず, jouzu）= good at |
| 下 | カ、ゲ | した、さ-、くだ- | down; below | 下手（へた, heta）= poor at |
| 中 | チュウ | なか | middle; inside | 中国（ちゅうごく, Chuugoku）= China |
| 外 | ガイ、ゲ | そと、ほか | outside | 外国人（がいこくじん, gaikokujin）= foreigner |
| 左 | サ | ひだり | left | 左（ひだり, hidari）= left |
| 右 | ウ、ユウ | みぎ | right | 右（みぎ, migi）= right |
| 東 | トウ | ひがし | east | 東京（とうきょう, Toukyou）= Tokyo |
| 西 | セイ、サイ | にし | west | 西（にし, nishi）= west |
| 南 | ナン | みなみ | south | 南口（みなみぐち, minamiguchi）= south exit |
| 北 | ホク | きた | north | 北口（きたぐち, kitaguchi）= north exit |

**Stroke counts:** 上 3, 下 3, 中 4, 外 5, 左 5, 右 5, 東 8, 西 6, 南 9, 北 5. **Radical idea:** 上 and 下 are pictures: a line with a marker *above* it (上) or *below* it (下). 中 is a line passing through the middle of a box — literally "through the centre". 外 is 夕 (evening) + 卜 (divination): old readings of "outside" came from evening fortune-telling done outdoors — a story, not a rule, so treat it as a hook only.

**The compass set:** 東西南北（とうざいなんぼく, touzai nanboku）is the standard four-direction phrase. 東京 = "eastern capital", 関西（かんさい, Kansai）= "west of the barrier".

**Example question:** 上手 is read じょうず and 下手 is read へた. Why does that matter for an exam answer?

**Solution:**

- Step 1: 上手 and 下手 are **not** read with their obvious readings (じょうしゅ / かしゅ). They are fixed whole words: じょうず (skilled) and へた (unskilled).
- Step 2: both are **na-adjectives**, so they attach to nouns with な and take が for the thing you are good at: 田中さんは 日本語が 上手です.
- Step 3: exam reason — the examiner is testing whether you know these as vocabulary, not as two characters read separately.

**Final answer:** じょうず = good at, へた = poor at; both na-adjectives taking が.

⚠️ **Common trap:** 上手 and 下手 are the single most mis-read pair in N5. Do not attempt to build their reading from the characters. Memorise the whole word.

| 書 | ショ | か- | to write | 辞書（じしょ, jisho）= dictionary |
| 読 | ドク、トク | よ- | to read | 読む（よむ, yomu）= to read |

**Stroke counts:** 学 8, 生 5, 先 6, 校 10, 本 5, 語 14, 国 8, 字 6, 書 10, 読 14. **Radical idea:** 語 and 読 both carry 言（ごんべん, speech radical）— language and reading are things you do with words. 校 carries 木 (tree) because the original meaning was a wooden frame or a school building. 字 has the roof radical 宀 with 子 (child) under it: a child under a roof, learning characters. 国 is an enclosure 囗 around 玉 (jewel).

**The 生 family — worth a minute:** 学生（がくせい, student）, 先生（せんせい, teacher）, 一生懸命 is too advanced, but 生まれる（うまれる, to be born）and 生活（せいかつ, daily life）show how wide 生 is. At N5, just learn 学生 and 先生 as fixed words.

**Example question:** Read 日本語, 英語（えいご, Eigo）and 辞書. Then explain the role of 語 in the first two.

**Solution:**

- 日本語 = 日本 (にほん) + 語 (ご) → にほんご, "Japan + language" = the Japanese language.
- 英語 = 英 (えい, England) + 語 (ご) → えいご, the English language.
- 辞書 = 辞 (じ) + 書 (しょ, write) → じしょ, "a book of words" = dictionary.
- 語 is a **suffix** meaning "language of X". Pattern: `country/people + 語 = that language`.

**Final answer:** にほんご / えいご / じしょ; 語 = "language of ___".

### Theme 9 — Everyday verbs and adjectives

| 漢字 | On-yomi | Kun-yomi | Meaning | Example word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 行 | コウ、ギョウ | い-、ゆ- | to go | 行く（いく, iku）= to go |
| 来 | ライ | く-、き- | to come | 来る（くる, kuru）= to come |
| 見 | ケン | み- | to see | 見る（みる, miru）= to see |
| 食 | ショク | た-、く- | to eat; food | 食べる（たべる, taberu）= to eat |
| 飲 | イン | の- | to drink | 飲む（のむ, nomu）= to drink |
| 買 | バイ | か- | to buy | 買い物（かいもの, kaimono）= shopping |
| 大 | ダイ、タイ | おお- | big | 大きい（おおきい, ookii）= big |
| 小 | ショウ | ちい-、こ- | small | 小さい（ちいさい, chiisai）= small |
| 高 | コウ | たか- | high; expensive | 高い（たかい, takai）= tall, expensive |
| 安 | アン | やす- | cheap; safe | 安い（やすい, yasui）= cheap |

**Stroke counts:** 行 6, 来 7, 見 7, 食 9, 飲 12, 買 12, 大 3, 小 3, 高 10, 安 6. **Radical idea:** 大 is a person with arms and legs spread wide — "big". 小 is three small strokes — "small". 見 is 目 (eye) on legs: an eye that walks around, i.e. seeing. 安 is a woman 女 under a roof 宀: safe at home.

**Why the kun-yomi ends in a dash:** い- in 行 means the reading is a **stem**, and a hiragana tail completes it: 行（い）く = いく. Never read a bare 行 as "i" on its own. Same for 見（み）る, 食（た）べる, 飲（の）む, 買（か）う.

**Example question:** Give the reading of 大きい, 大学 and 大人（おとな）. Why is 大 read differently in each?

**Solution:**

- 大きい → 大 is a standalone adjective stem + きい tail → kun-yomi おお- → おおきい.
- 大学 → kanji + kanji compound → on-yomi ダイ + ガク → だいがく.
- 大人 → a fixed word with a special reading: おとな. Neither ダイ nor おお- applies; it is an exceptional whole-word reading (literally "big person").

**Final answer:** おおきい / だいがく / おとな. Rule of thumb: alone or with a kana tail → kun-yomi; inside a kanji compound → on-yomi; some very common words are simply irregular and must be memorised.

## 1.2 Essential vocabulary

### Intuition

Vocabulary is not a list you memorise once — it is a **set of keys you keep jingling**. You do not "learn" みず (mizu, water) by staring at it. You learn it by meeting it five times in five different situations: on a bottle, in a sentence, in a shop, in your own writing, in a dialogue. The notes below give you the words grouped by *situation* so your brain stores them together, which is how you will actually need them.

### The idea, step by step

**Step 1 — always store three things per word:** the kana (how it sounds), the romaji (your crutch until you can read kana fluently), and the meaning *in a sentence context*. A word without a context is a word you will drop.

**Step 2 — spaced repetition.** Review a new word **the same day, then after 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 14 days, 30 days**. Each time you recall it, the memory gets cheaper to hold. Practical version for a B.Tech schedule: 15 new words a day in a paper notebook, and every evening you write yesterday's 15 from memory. Ten minutes, not an hour.

## Solved examples

**Example 1 (easy).** Read this shop sign: たまご 十個 百五十円.

- たまご = tamago, egg.
- 十 = じゅう, ten. 個（こ）is a counter for small loose items, so 十個 = じゅっこ (jukko).
- 百五十円: 百 = ひゃく, 五十 = ごじゅう, 円 = えん → ひゃくごじゅうえん.
- **Answer:** ten eggs, 150 yen — たまご じゅっこ、ひゃくごじゅうえん.

**Example 2 (medium).** Write "My older sister is a university student in Tokyo" using only characters and patterns you have met.

- "older sister" (my own) → あね.
- Topic marker は.
- "university student" → だいがくせい. 東京 = とうきょう. "in Tokyo" → とうきょうに (location of living) or simply state it: とうきょうの だいがくせい (a university student *of* Tokyo).
- **Answer:** あねは とうきょうの だいがくせいです（ane wa Toukyou no daigakusei desu）.

## Common traps and mistakes

- **Reading 四月 as よんがつ.** Months are しがつ, しちがつ, くがつ — the "unpopular" readings come back for months. Costs marks in every date question.
- **Reading 上手 / 下手 character by character.** They are じょうず / へた as whole words. This is a favourite exam trap.
- **Mixing 父 and お父さん.** Using お父さん about your own father to a stranger sounds like you are praising your own family; using 父 about someone else's father is rude. Costs marks in any dialogue-completion question.
- **Putting に after あした / きょう / まいにち.** Relative time words take no particle. Very common slip.
- **Counting people with つ.** 三人 is さんにん, never みっつ. Counter choice is a meaning question, not a style question.
- **Writing a kanji with the wrong stroke count.** 時 is 10 strokes, not 9. If a "write the kanji" question is marked on shape, a missing stroke is a missing stroke.
- **Learning readings without a word.** 生 has many readings; せい in 先生, い in 生きる, う in 生まれる, なま in 生. Learning the character without the word means you cannot choose.
- **Trusting "ends in い = i-adjective".** きれい and げんき are na-adjectives. Test with な.

## Quick revision box

✅ **80 kanji, 9 themes:** numbers (10), money/big numbers (5), days & time (12), family (9), nature (9), body (5), directions (10), school & language (10), verbs & adjectives (10).
✅ **On-yomi = katakana = in compounds. Kun-yomi = hiragana = alone or with a kana tail.**
✅ **Stroke order:** top→bottom, left→right, horizontal before vertical, outside before inside, dots last.
✅ **Radicals carry meaning:** 言 = speech (語, 読), 木 = tree (校), 日 = sun (時), 艹 = grass (花), 宀 = roof (字), 囗 = enclosure (国).
✅ **Numbers group by four zeros:** 万 = 10,000, so 25,000 = 二万五千.
✅ **Fixed irregular readings to chant:** ひとつ ふたつ みっつ よっつ いつつ むっつ ななつ やっつ ここのつ とお; ついたち ふつか みっか よっか いつか むいか なのか ようか ここのか とおか はつか.
✅ **Counters:** つ (loose things), 人 (people: ひとり, ふたり, よにん), 枚 (flat), 本 (long thin: いっぽん, さんぼん, ろっぽん), 匹 (small animals: いっぴき, さんびき).
✅ **Vocabulary plan:** 15 words a day, review at 1/3/7/14/30 days, always with a particle and a sentence.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** Read these and give the meaning: 三千円、八つ、五月、十分.

**Q2.** Which reading (on or kun) is used in each: 日本の 日、日曜日の 日、日の 日?

**Q3.** Fill in the correct counter: ペン 3 ___、ねこ 2 ___、かみ 5 ___、学生 4 ___.

**Q4.** A sign says 出口 and another says 入口. Which is the exit, and how is each read?

**Q5.** Your friend asks about your family. Say "My father is a teacher" and then "My mother is not a teacher" — in Japanese.

**Q6.** Write the date 3 March 2024 in kanji and read it aloud. Watch the month and day readings.

## Answers

**A1.** さんぜんえん (3,000 yen — note 千 voices to ぜん after 三); やっつ (eight things); ごがつ (May); じゅっぷん (ten minutes — 分 becomes ぷん after 十).

**A2.** 日本 → on-yomi (に, from ニチ); 日曜日 → on-yomi (にち); 日 alone → kun-yomi (ひ). Key step: kanji compound = on-yomi, standing alone = kun-yomi.

**A3.** ペン 3本（さんぼん）、ねこ 2匹（にひき）、かみ 5枚（ごまい）、学生 4人（よにん）. Key step: counter follows the shape, and 4人 is the irregular よにん.

**A4.** 出口 = でぐち is the exit; 入口 = いりぐち is the entrance. Key step: 口 voices to ぐち in both compounds.

**A5.** ちちは せんせいです（chichi wa sensei desu）. ははは せんせいじゃありません（haha wa sensei ja arimasen）. Key step: use ちち / はは for your own parents, and じゃありません for the negative.

**A6.** 二千二十四年三月三日（にせんにじゅうよねん さんがつ みっか）. Key steps: 三月 = さんがつ (not さんげつ); 三日 = みっか, one of the irregular first-ten dates; 二十四 = にじゅうよん.
