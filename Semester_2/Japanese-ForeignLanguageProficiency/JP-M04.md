# Japanese (Foreign Language Proficiency) – Module 4
## The Four Skills: Reading, Listening, Speaking, Writing
*Friend-style notes: intuition first, then the pattern, then a small example.*

## The big picture

Units 1–3 built a stock of words, a grammar engine, and the patterns for talking about yourself. Unit 4 changes the question. It is no longer "do you know the pattern?" but "can you *use* it under time pressure, in a real text, at real speed?" That is a different skill, and it is trained separately.

One analogy: until now you were learning to drive in an empty car park — perfect conditions, no clock, no traffic. Unit 4 puts you on the road. Same car. Now there are signs to read, a passenger talking to you, and someone waiting for your answer.

The syllabus describes this unit in three layers, and these notes cover all of them:

| Syllabus layer | Covered in |
|---|---|
| 4.1 Skills developed — Reading, Listening, Speaking, Writing | sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.4 |
| 4.2 Through — practical conversations, short passages, notices, simple writing tasks | the model texts and scripts in 4.1–4.4 |
| 4.3 Scope — familiar topics, JLPT N5 level | section 4.5 |

One honest point before we start. The real JLPT N5 exam has **three** papers: 言語知識（文字・語彙） (language knowledge: characters and vocabulary), 言語知識（文法）・読解 (grammar and reading), and 聴解 (listening). There is **no speaking section and no writing section** in the JLPT itself — it is a multiple-choice test. Speaking and writing are in *your course* syllabus because a language course tests production too. So treat 4.3 and 4.4 as course requirements that also make 4.1 and 4.2 easier, not as JLPT papers.

## Basics you must know first

- **N5 scope** — about 800 vocabulary items and 80 kanji, plus the grammar of Units 1–3. Anything outside that is not examinable, so do not panic about unknown words in a passage.
- **The です/ます sentence** — わたしは 学生です (I am a student). Topic は … comment … です. Almost every N5 reading sentence is this shape or the verb version.
- **Plain vs polite** — signs and notices are often plain or noun-only (立入禁止), letters and emails are polite (ます/です). Reading both is required.
- **Particles as signposts** — は (topic), が (subject), を (object), に (time/place), で (place of action), から/まで (from/until). In a passage, find the particle and you have found the sentence's job.
- **Katakana** — names, loanwords, and many place names appear in katakana: スーパー (supermarket), カフェ (café), パーティー (party).
- **Romaji here is Hepburn** with macrons for long vowels (がっこう = gakkō), and っ doubling the next consonant (きって = kitte).

## 4.1 Reading

### Intuition

An N5 reading passage is not a literature exam. It is a **treasure hunt with signposts**. Every sentence tells you who, where, when, or how much — and the question only ever asks for one of those four. So you do not read the passage and then think; you read the *question* first, decide which of the four you need, and hunt for it. The passage is kana-heavy and short (usually 100–200 characters), so hunting is fast.

Notices and signs are the other half of N5 reading: 入口, 立入禁止, 営業時間. These are not sentences at all — they are labels. Learn them as pictures, not as grammar.

### The idea, step by step

**Step 1 — the sign and notice vocabulary.** Learn these as blocks; they appear in every N5 paper.

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 入口 | いりぐち | entrance |
| 出口 | でぐち | exit |
| 立入禁止 | たちいりきんし | no entry / keep out |
| 禁止 | きんし | prohibited |
| 禁煙 | きんえん | no smoking |
| 営業時間 | えいぎょうじかん | business hours |
| 定休日 | ていきゅうび | regular closing day |
| 非常口 | ひじょうぐち | emergency exit |
| お手洗い | おてあらい | toilet |
| 受付 | うけつけ | reception |
| 無料 | むりょう | free of charge |
| 有料 | ゆうりょう | charged / not free |
| 使用禁止 | しようきんし | out of use |
| 止まれ | とまれ | stop (road sign) |

Two reading traps live in this table. 立入禁止 contains 入 (enter) but means the **opposite** — you may not enter. 非常口 contains 非常 (emergency) — it is not a normal exit. Signs are read by whole shape, which is why learning them as blocks beats decoding them.

**Step 2 — a notice in the wild.** A shop door says:

```text
営業時間　午前十時〜午後七時
定休日　水曜日
```

午前 (gozen) = a.m., 午後 (gogo) = p.m., 〜 = "to" in a range. So: open 10:00–19:00, closed Wednesdays. The question will be something like 水曜日に この みせは あいて いますか。 (Is this shop open on Wednesday?) Answer: いいえ、あいて いません。定休日ですから。 — because 定休日 means the regular closing day. (あいて います, aite imasu = is open, from あく, to open.)

**Step 3 — the attack plan for an unseen passage.** Four moves, in this order:

1. **Read the question first.** It tells you what to hunt: a person (だれ), a place (どこ), a time (いつ/なんじ), a thing (なに), or a number (いくら/いくつ).
2. **Skim for katakana and numbers.** Names and prices are visually loud; they anchor you in the passage.
3. **Find the sentence with the answer, not the whole passage.** N5 answers sit in one sentence, usually near the end.
4. **Check the polarity of that sentence.** Is it です or じゃありません, ます or ません? N5 questions love the negative.

**Step 4 — a full model passage with questions.** Read it once without stopping; you are allowed not to know every word.

```text
わたしの まち

わたしは 田中です。わたしの まちは 小さな まちです。えきの ちかくに
スーパーが あります。スーパーの となりに 本やが あります。本やの
うしろに こうえんが あります。こうえんは しずかで、きれいです。
わたしは まいにち こうえんを さんぽします。日曜日は こうえんの
ちかくの カフェで コーヒーを のみます。カフェは やすくて、おいしいです。

My town
I am Tanaka. My town is a small town. Near the station there is a supermarket.
Next to the supermarket there is a bookshop. Behind the bookshop there is a park.
The park is quiet and clean. I walk in the park every day. On Sundays I drink
coffee at the café near the park. The café is cheap and delicious.
```

New words in it: まち (machi, town), ちかく (chikaku, nearby), となり (tonari, next to), うしろ (ushiro, behind), こうえん (kōen, park), さんぽします (sanpo shimasu, take a walk), やすくて (yasukute, cheap and…).

Now the questions, answered the way you would in an exam:

**Q1. 本やは どこに ありますか。** (Where is the bookshop?)
Hunt for 本や. The sentence is スーパーの となりに 本やが あります — "next to the supermarket". Answer: スーパーの となりです。

**Q2. 日曜日に なにを しますか。** (What does he do on Sundays?)
Hunt for 日曜日. Sentence: 日曜日は … カフェで コーヒーを のみます。Answer: カフェで コーヒーを のみます。

**Q3. カフェは どんな みせですか。** (What kind of shop is the café?)
Hunt for カフェ in the last sentence: やすくて、おいしいです。Answer: やすくて、おいしい みせです。

Notice that Q1's answer required the particle に (location of existence), Q2's required the で (place of action), and Q3's required the て-form joining two adjectives. The grammar from Unit 2 is doing all the work — reading is applied grammar.

**Step 5 — emails and letters.** A short N5 email has a fixed skeleton: greeting → body → request → closing.

```text
田中さん
おはよう ございます。マリです。
あした、いっしょに えいがを 見ませんか。えいがは 六時からです。
えきの まえで あいましょう。
へんじを ください。              マリ

Good morning, Tanaka. This is Mari. Won't you watch a film with us tomorrow?
The film starts at six. Let's meet in front of the station. Please reply. — Mari
```

Three things to notice: the addressee's name sits alone on the first line (no へ needed, though 田中さんへ is also fine); へんじを ください (henji o kudasai, please reply) is the standard request line; the sender's name goes at the end.

### Formula / rule / table

```text
Notice reading: find the noun, ignore the grammar.
Passage reading: question → katakana/numbers → one answer sentence → check polarity.
```

| Question word | What to hunt for | Particle clue |
|---|---|---|
| だれ (who) | a person's name | は / が |
| どこ (where) | a place noun | に あります / で |
| いつ・なんじ (when) | a time or day | に / から / まで |
| なに (what) | a thing noun | を |
| いくら (how much) | a number + えん | は |

**Example question:** Read the notice and answer.

```text
プール
営業時間　午前九時〜午後六時
定休日　月曜日
おとなは 入れません。子供だけです。
```

Question: 大人は この プールで およぐ ことが できますか。 (Can an adult swim in this pool?)

**Solution:**
Step 1 — read the question first: it asks about 大人 (otona, adult) and およぐ (oyogu, swim), so it is a yes/no about permission.
Step 2 — hunt for おとな (otona, adult) in the notice. It appears in おとなは 入れません — 入れる (haireru) is the potential form of 入る (hairu, enter), so 入れません = cannot enter.
Step 3 — the negative is at the very end of that sentence, which is exactly where N5 hides it.
Step 4 — 子供だけです (kodomo dake desu, children only) confirms the restriction, so an adult cannot swim there. Answer is negative.
**Final answer:** いいえ、できません。子供だけですから。 (No, they cannot. Because it is children only.)

⚠️ **Common trap:** in a notice, a word that *looks* positive can be a prohibition. 立入禁止 and 使用禁止 both contain no negative verb at all, yet both mean "do not". Never translate a sign word-by-word; learn the block. Second trap: answering from general knowledge instead of the text — the passage says what it says, even if real life differs.

## 4.2 Listening

### Intuition

Listening at N5 is not about understanding every word. It is about catching **one piece of information** while people talk at natural speed — and the test deliberately puts a decoy in the way. Someone says a number, then corrects it. Someone says "coffee", then says "actually, tea". The skill is: hold the question in your head, and let the decoys bounce off.

Think of it as a cricket commentary. You do not follow every sentence; you wait for "six" or "out". At N5 the "six" is a time, a price, a place, or a yes/no.

### The idea, step by step

**Step 1 — the four N5 listening task types.**

| Task type | Japanese name | What it tests | How to play it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task comprehension | 課題理解 (kadai rikai) | "What must the person do next?" | Note the **action** the speaker is told to take |
| Point comprehension | ポイント理解 (pointo rikai) | "Which one / what time / how much?" | Note the **single detail** asked about |
| Utterance expression | 発話表現 (hatsuwa hyōgen) | "What would you say here?" (with a picture) | Pick the phrase that fits the **situation and politeness** |
| Quick response | 即時応答 (sokuji ōtō) | A one-line reply to a one-line remark | Judge the **relationship and the mood** |

In 課題理解 and ポイント理解 you hear the question **before** the dialogue, so write it down in Japanese as you hear it. In 発話表現 and 即時応答 you see or hear a short prompt and choose from three printed options — the audio is short, so the decision must be instant.

**Step 2 — the four traps that decide marks.**

1. **Numbers and counters.** The sound changes are what get tested, not the arithmetic.

| Number | Reading | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 4時 | よじ | not しじ |
| 7時 | しちじ | also said ななじ in speech |
| 9時 | くじ | not きゅうじ |
| 1分 | いっぷん | small っ |
| 3分 | さんぷん | |
| 4分 | よんぷん | |
| 6分 | ろっぷん | small っ |
| 8分 | はっぷん | small っ |
| 10分 | じゅっぷん | small っ |
| 300 | さんびゃく | |
| 600 | ろっぴゃく | |
| 800 | はっぴゃく | |
| 3000 | さんぜん | |
| 8000 | はっせん | |

2. **Negation at the very end.** Japanese puts the verb last, so a sentence sounds positive until its final syllable: 行きます (I'll go) versus 行きません (I won't go). If you stop listening early, you write the opposite answer.
3. **Correction in mid-sentence.** ええと、九時です。あ、すみません、十時でした。 — the second number wins. でした (was) after a correction is your signal.
4. **Softeners that mean "no".** ちょっと… (a bit…), けっこうです (I'm fine, thanks), いいです (often "no thanks" when said about an offer), 大丈夫です (daijōbu desu, I'm okay = no). A polite refusal at N5 almost never contains the word いいえ.

**Step 3 — a practice script (課題理解 style).** Read the question first, then the transcript.

Question: 男の人は 何時までに 来ますか。 (By what time does the man have to come?)

```text
男：すみません、あしたの テストは 何時からですか。
女：ええと、九時からです。でも、八時半までに 来て ください。
男：わかりました。あ、テストは どこですか。
女：三かいの 302の きょうしつです。
男：ありがとう ございます。

Man: Excuse me, what time does tomorrow's test start?
Woman: Let me see, from nine. But please come by 8:30.
Man: Understood. Oh, where is the test?
Woman: Room 302 on the third floor.
Man: Thank you very much.
```

**Step 4 — working the transcript like an exam.** Three observations:

- 九時から is a **decoy**: it is when the test starts, not when he must arrive. The question asks までに (by), and the answer is 八時半.
- でも (demo, but) marks the correction. Whatever follows でも is the operative instruction.
- 三かいの 302 is the answer to a *different* question (where), so it is noise for this question. This is why you must fix the question in your head first.

Answer: 八時半までに 来ます。

**Step 5 — a quick-response item.** You hear one line and choose the reply.

```text
女：いっしょに ひるごはんを たべませんか。
(a) ええ、たべました。
(b) いいですね。行きましょう。
(c) いいえ、たべません。
```

(b) is correct: an invitation is answered with agreement plus ましょう. (a) answers a past-tense question that was not asked. (c) is a flat refusal of an invitation — grammatically possible but socially wrong, and N5 items reward the natural reply.

**Step 6 — a point-comprehension item with a softly hidden "no".**

```text
女：のどが かわきましたね。コーヒーを のみませんか。
男：コーヒーは ちょっと…。こうちゃが いいです。

Woman: I'm thirsty. Shall we have coffee?
Man: Coffee is a bit… Tea would be good.
```

Question: 男の人は 何を のみますか。 The trap is ちょっと…, which is a refusal, not a hesitation. He drinks こうちゃ (kōcha, tea). If you miss the softener you answer コーヒー and lose the mark.

### Formula / rule / table

```text
Listen for: the question first → the detail → the final verb → any でも correction.
A polite "no" is rarely いいえ: ちょっと… / けっこうです / だいじょうぶです.
```

| Sound in the audio | What it signals | Do |
|---|---|---|
| でも / あ、すみません | correction coming | discard the earlier number |
| ～ました (after a number) | past/corrected figure | take the new figure |
| ちょっと… | refusal | answer "no / the other option" |
| までに | deadline | do not answer with the start time |
| から | start point | do not answer with the deadline |
| final ません / じゃない | negative | flip your answer |

**Example question:** You hear:

```text
男：あした、えいがを 見ませんか。
女：あしたは ちょっと…。あさっては どうですか。
男：いいですね。じゃ、あさって。
```

Question: 二人は いつ えいがを 見ますか。 (When will they see the film?)

**Solution:**
Step 1 — fix the question: it asks **when**, so track only time words.
Step 2 — あした appears first (tomorrow) — but it is attached to ちょっと…, which is a refusal, so あした is dead.
Step 3 — あさって (asatte, the day after tomorrow) appears with どうですか, a counter-suggestion, and the man accepts with いいですね。
Step 4 — the acceptance sentence じゃ、あさって repeats the agreed day, confirming it.
**Final answer:** あさって 見ます。 (They will watch it the day after tomorrow.)

⚠️ **Common trap:** two things sink marks here. First, answering with the **first** time/number you hear — N5 scripts are built so the first figure is a decoy. Second, stopping your attention at the last noun; the verb that decides positive/negative comes *after* it. Train yourself to hold the sentence open until the verb lands.

## 4.3 Speaking

### Intuition

The N5-style speaking test is a **conversation, not a performance**. An examiner asks simple questions about your life and listens for whether you can answer in a complete, polite sentence without freezing. Fluency at N5 means *not stopping*, not *being impressive*. A short correct sentence delivered calmly scores better than a long one you abandon halfway.

So the whole strategy is: **prepare blocks, not sentences.** You will be asked about yourself, your day, your likes, your family. Each of those is a block you can rehearse until it comes out automatically, and then you assemble answers from blocks.

### The idea, step by step

**Step 1 — the shape of the interview.** Typically four phases:

| Phase | What the examiner says | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | おはよう ございます。すわって ください。 | greet back, sit, stay polite |
| Self-introduction | じこしょうかいを して ください。 | your prepared じこしょうかい |
| Questions | しゅみは？ かぞくは？ まいにち 何を しますか。 | answer in full sentences |
| Closing | ありがとう ございました。 | ありがとう ございました。しつれいします。 |

すわって ください (suwatte kudasai) = please sit; しつれいします (shitsurei shimasu) = excuse me / goodbye-polite.

**Step 2 — the model self-introduction (じこしょうかい).** Learn this as a script, then swap the details for your own.

```text
はじめまして。わたしは ラジャと いいます。
インドの デリーから きました。
だいがくの いちねんせいです。せんこうは コンピューター・サイエンスです。
しゅみは おんがくです。よく おんがくを ききます。
かぞくは 四人です。ちちと ははと あねと わたしです。
にほんごを まいにち すこし べんきょうしています。
どうぞ よろしく おねがいします。

Hajimemashite. Watashi wa Raja to iimasu.
Indo no Derī kara kimashita.
Daigaku no ichinensei desu. Senkō wa konpyūtā saisensu desu.
Shumi wa ongaku desu. Yoku ongaku o kikimasu.
Kazoku wa yonin desu. Chichi to haha to ane to watashi desu.
Nihongo o mainichi sukoshi benkyō shite imasu.
Dōzo yoroshiku onegaishimasu.

How do you do. My name is Raja.
I came from Delhi, India.
I am a first-year university student. My major is computer science.
My hobby is music. I often listen to music.
My family is four people: my father, my mother, my elder sister and me.
I am studying a little Japanese every day.
Pleased to meet you.
```

Five things this script gets right, and why each matters:

- はじめまして (hajimemashite) opens; どうぞ よろしく おねがいします closes. A self-introduction without the closing line sounds unfinished.
- ～と いいます (to iimasu, "am called") is the standard way to give your name, from 3.7.
- から きました (kara kimashita, "came from") — から marks origin.
- せんこう (senkō) = major, しゅみ (shumi) = hobby. Two words that carry a lot of the interview.
- 四人 is よにん (yonin), not よんにん.

**Step 3 — family words, because they will ask.** In Japanese you use **humble** words for your own family and **polite** words for someone else's. At N5 you only need your own set.

| Your family | Reading | Meaning | (Someone else's) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ちち | chichi | my father | おとうさん |
| はは | haha | my mother | おかあさん |
| あに | ani | my elder brother | おにいさん |
| あね | ane | my elder sister | おねえさん |
| おとうと | otōto | my younger brother | おとうとさん |
| いもうと | imōto | my younger sister | いもうとさん |

So: ちちは かいしゃいんです (my father is a company employee), ははは せんせいです (my mother is a teacher). Never say おとうさん about your own father to an outsider — it sounds as if you are talking about someone else's.

**Step 4 — describing your day and your likes on the spot.** Answer in **three parts**: direct answer → one detail → one extra sentence. That gives you length without risk.

```text
Q: まいにち 何を しますか。
A: まいにち がっこうへ 行きます。あさ 八時に うちを 出ます。
   よるは うちで べんきょうします。

Q: どんな たべものが すきですか。
A: カレーが すきです。インドの カレーは とても おいしいです。
   でも、なっとうは あまり すきじゃありません。

Q: にほんごは むずかしいですか。
A: すこし むずかしいですが、おもしろいです。かんじが すきです。
```

Note the third answer's shape: むずかしいですが、おもしろいです — an い-adjective with が as "but", straight from Unit 2. Examiners reward that structure because it shows range.

**Step 5 — the three things that lose marks.**

1. **Answering in one word.** はい、カレー。 is not an answer; カレーが すきです。 is.
2. **Freezing on an unknown word.** Use the escape hatch: すみません、わかりません。もう いちど おねがいします。 (Sorry, I don't understand. Once more, please.) That is polite, correct, and keeps the conversation alive.
3. **Dropping politeness under pressure.** Keep です/ます throughout. Plain forms are for friends.

### Formula / rule / table

```text
Answer = direct answer + one detail + one extra sentence.
Self-intro order: greeting → name → origin → status → hobby → family → study → closing.
```

| You are asked | Start your answer with |
|---|---|
| おなまえは？ | わたしは ～と いいます。 |
| どこから きましたか。 | ～から きました。 |
| しゅみは なんですか。 | しゅみは ～です。 |
| かぞくは なんにんですか。 | ～人です。 |
| まいにち 何を しますか。 | まいにち ～します。 |
| ～が すきですか。 | はい、～が すきです。/ いいえ、あまり すきじゃありません。 |

**Example question:** The examiner asks しゅみは なんですか。 You have no hobby you can name in Japanese. Give the best possible answer in three parts, and say why it beats staying silent.

**Solution:**
Step 1 — you must answer *something*, because silence scores zero and a wrong-but-fluent answer scores more than nothing.
Step 2 — pick a hobby you *can* say. おんがく (ongaku, music), スポーツ (supōtsu), えいが (eiga, film), ほん (hon, books) are all N5 words.
Step 3 — give part 1, the direct answer: しゅみは おんがくです。
Step 4 — add a detail: よく おんがくを ききます。 (I often listen to music.)
Step 5 — add an extra sentence to show range: にほんの おんがくも すきです。 (I like Japanese music too.)
Step 6 — if you genuinely cannot, use the escape hatch instead: すみません、わかりません。 — but a rehearsed answer is always better.
**Final answer:** しゅみは おんがくです。よく おんがくを ききます。にほんの おんがくも すきです。

⚠️ **Common trap:** the biggest speaking trap is translating in your head from English. It produces long, broken sentences. Say the short Japanese sentence you *own* — おんがくが すきです — rather than attempting a translation of a complicated English thought. Accuracy and calmness are what the marks are for, not ambition.

## 4.4 Writing

### Intuition

N5 writing is four tiny text types: a self-introduction, a diary entry, a short message/email, and a postcard. All four are short, all four are polite, and all four use the same few sentence shapes you already know. Nothing needs to be creative. What is being tested is **control**: correct particles, correct endings, correct punctuation, and readable kanji.

Think of it as handwriting practice with grammar attached. A clean, simple, correct paragraph beats an ambitious broken one every time.

### The idea, step by step

**Step 1 — punctuation: 。 and 、**

- 。 (句点, kuten) ends a sentence. It is a small circle, not a dot.
- 、 (読点, tōten) separates clauses and list items — the Japanese comma.
- 「 」 (kagi kakko) wraps quoted speech: 「ありがとう」と いいました。
- ？ is used in casual writing and questions in some textbooks, but in polite formal writing 。 often replaces it: おげんきですか。 — that is correct and normal.

Rules that get marked:

| Rule | Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| No spaces between words | わたしは学生です。 | わたし は 学生 です。 |
| Comma after a long opening phrase | あしたは、テストが あります。 | (no comma, hard to read) |
| No comma before です/ます | これは ほんです。 | これは、ほんです。 |
| One 。 per sentence | 行きます。たべます。 | 行きます、たべます。 |

**Step 2 — kanji or kana?** You have ~80 kanji; use them where you know them, and kana everywhere else. Two hard rules:

- **Particles are always kana**: は, を, へ, が, に, で, と, も. Never write を as 男 or は as 波 — those are different words.
- **Okurigana stays**: the kana part of a mixed word is written in kana. 食べます (not 食ます), 大きい (not 大い), 書きます (not 書ます).

Common N5 words that are usually written in kana even though kanji exist: ください, いる/ある as helpers, そして, それから, とても, すこし, たくさん. Writing them in kana is not an error — it is normal.

**Step 3 — model 1: self-introduction (じこしょうかい).**

```text
はじめまして。わたしは ラジャです。インドから きました。
だいがくの いちねんせいです。しゅみは おんがくです。
どうぞ よろしく おねがいします。
```

Three sentences, three jobs: who you are, what you are, what you like. That is a complete N5 self-introduction.

**Step 4 — model 2: diary entry (にっき).** A diary is written in plain/polite past, with the date at the top.

```text
七月十日（月）
きょうは あさから あめでした。ごご、ともだちと えいがを 見ました。
えいがは とても おもしろかったです。よるは うちで カレーを たべました。
たのしかったです。

10 July (Mon)
It rained from the morning today. In the afternoon I watched a film with a
friend. The film was very interesting. In the evening I ate curry at home.
It was fun.
```

Grammar points doing the work: あめでした (noun + でした, past), 見ました (polite past), おもしろかったです (い-adjective past: い → かった + です), たのしかったです (same rule). The い-adjective past is the one thing students forget in a diary.

**Step 5 — model 3: a short message or email.**

```text
田中さん
あしたの パーティーは 七時からです。えきの まえで あいましょう。
へんじを ください。              ラジャ

Tanaka — Tomorrow's party starts at seven. Let's meet in front of the station.
Please reply. — Raja
```

Skeleton: addressee on line 1 → time/place information → あいましょう (proposal) → へんじを ください (request) → your name at the end.

**Step 6 — model 4: a postcard (はがき).** A postcard is shorter, more personal, and often in the past tense about a trip.

```text
田中さん
おげんきですか。わたしは げんきです。きょう、ふじさんに のぼりました。
とても きれいでした。あしたは とうきょうへ 行きます。
七月十日　　ラジャ

Tanaka — How are you? I am well. Today I climbed Mt Fuji. It was very
beautiful. Tomorrow I will go to Tokyo. — 10 July, Raja
```

Note きれいでした: きれい looks like an い-adjective but is a **な-adjective**, so its past is でした, not きれかったです. That single word is the most common writing error at N5.

**Step 7 — the N5 writing checklist.** Before you hand anything in, run these seven checks:

1. Every sentence ends in です/ます (or a plain form, if the task is a diary in plain style — be consistent).
2. Every verb and adjective agrees in tense: past events get ました/でした/かったです.
3. Particles: は for topic, が for subject, を for object, に for time, で for place of action.
4. い-adjective past = ～かったです; な-adjective past = ～でした.
5. Punctuation: 。 at every sentence end, 、 after long openings, no spaces.
6. Kanji you know, kana for the rest, particles always kana.
7. Name and date where the format expects them (top of a diary, end of a message).

### Formula / rule / table

```text
Diary: date → past-tense sentences → feeling sentence.
Message: addressee → information → proposal → へんじを ください → name.
Postcard: addressee → greeting → news (past) → next plan → date + name.
```

| Text type | Tense | Politeness | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| じこしょうかい | present | polite | 3–5 sentences |
| にっき | past | polite or plain (be consistent) | 4–6 sentences |
| メール / メッセージ | present + proposal | polite | 2–4 lines |
| はがき | past + future | polite | 3–5 sentences |

**Example question:** Fix the four errors in this diary entry.

```text
七月十日
きょうは あめです。ごご、ともだちと えいがを 見ます。
えいがは とても おもしろいでした。たのしかったです。
```

**Solution:**
Step 1 — the date says 七月十日 and the entry describes today, which is finished, so the events must be **past**. あめです → あめでした. (Error 1)
Step 2 — 見ます → 見ました, same reason: the film was watched this afternoon. (Error 2)
Step 3 — おもしろいでした is impossible: おもしろい is an い-adjective, and い-adjectives never take でした. The past is おもしろかったです. (Error 3)
Step 4 — たのしかったです is already correct (い → かった + です), so leave it.
Step 5 — one punctuation check: the sentences are correctly closed with 。 and the opening きょうは needs no comma because it is short.
**Final answer:**
七月十日
きょうは あめでした。ごご、ともだちと えいがを 見ました。
えいがは とても おもしろかったです。たのしかったです。

⚠️ **Common trap:** two errors dominate N5 writing. First, mixing tenses inside one text — the date fixes the tense, so a diary about today cannot contain です/ます present forms for finished events. Second, い-adjectives with でした: おもしろいでした, たかいでした, きれいかったです are all wrong. Remember: **い → かったです; な/noun → でした.**

## 4.5 Practical scope

### Intuition

"Familiar topics only" is not a limitation to complain about — it is a gift. It means the exam cannot surprise you with content. It can only surprise you with **form**. So revision is not "learn more Japanese"; it is "make the Japanese you already have automatic". Familiar topics means: yourself, family, school/work, food, shopping, travel, weather, daily routine, likes.

### The idea, step by step

**Step 1 — the topic list you must be able to handle in all four skills.**

| Topic | Reading | Listening | Speaking | Writing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Self & family | name, age, job | who is who | じこしょうかい | self-intro paragraph |
| Daily routine | timetable | times | まいにち 何を しますか | diary entry |
| Food | menu, prices | ordering | すき/きらい | what you ate |
| Shopping | price tags, 無料/有料 | how much | いくらですか | a message about a purchase |
| Travel | signs, 営業時間 | platform, time | where you went | postcard |
| Weather | forecast words | あめ/はれ | きょうは あついですね | diary line |
| Likes | short passage | preference | すきです | hobby sentence |

If you can do every cell in that table at N5 level, you are ready. Nothing outside it will appear.

**Step 2 — how to revise each skill.**

- **Kanji (80):** write each one 10 times by hand while saying its reading aloud. Reading aloud matters — recognition alone fails in listening.
- **Vocabulary (800):** group by topic, not alphabetically. Learn たべもの words together, のりもの words together.
- **Grammar:** one pattern per day, and always produce two sentences of your own with it. Reading a pattern is not learning it.
- **Listening:** 15 minutes daily of N5 audio, with the transcript. Listen once blind, then read the transcript, then listen again. The third pass is where learning happens.
- **Reading:** one short passage per day, timed at 3 minutes, using the four-move attack from 4.1.
- **Writing:** one 4-sentence text per day, rotating the four text types.

**Step 3 — the 4-week plan.** This assumes 1.5–2 hours a day; compress proportionally if you have less.

| Week | Focus | Daily work | Weekly check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kanji + vocabulary base | 10 new kanji/day (write + say), 20 words/day, 15 min listening | write 40 kanji from memory; 100-word vocab quiz |
| 2 | Grammar consolidation | one Unit 2/3 pattern per day + 2 own sentences; 1 short passage/day | 20-sentence self-test on particles |
| 3 | The four skills | 1 passage + 1 listening set + 1 written text + 5 min speaking aloud daily | full mock reading + listening paper |
| 4 | Mock exams + repair | one timed mock every second day; revise only what you got wrong | two full mocks, review every error |

**Step 4 — how to use a mock exam properly.** Do not just score it. For every wrong answer, write one line: *which* skill failed — the word, the particle, the tense, or the speed. Four mocks with honest error logs beat ten mocks without them, because the log tells you what to revise.

Also know the pass shape: N5 is scored out of 180, with 60 for each of the three papers (language knowledge/vocabulary, grammar+reading, listening), and you need both an overall pass mark and a minimum in each section — so a very weak listening score cannot be rescued by a strong vocabulary score. Check the current official JLPT guide for the exact numbers before your exam, since they are set by the administering body and I would not want you to rely on a remembered figure.

### Formula / rule / table

```text
Revision = make known Japanese automatic, not learn new Japanese.
Daily: 10 kanji + 20 words + 1 pattern + 1 passage + 15 min listening.
```

| Skill | Daily minimum | Best single exercise |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | 1 short passage | question-first hunt |
| Listening | 15 min | listen → transcript → listen again |
| Speaking | 5 min aloud | recorded じこしょうかい |
| Writing | 1 short text | 4-sentence diary |
| Kanji/vocab | 10 + 20 items | write and say aloud |

**Example question:** You have 20 days left, 1 hour a day, and your mock shows reading 70%, listening 40%, grammar 65%. What do you do with the 20 hours?

**Solution:**
Step 1 — the weakest section is listening at 40%, and the JLPT has a sectional minimum, so listening is the actual risk to a pass, not the total.
Step 2 — allocate by weakness, not equally: about 10 hours to listening, 4 to grammar, 4 to reading, 2 to vocabulary maintenance.
Step 3 — listening work must be transcript-based (blind → transcript → blind), because passive listening without feedback does not move a 40% score.
Step 4 — keep a small daily kanji/vocab drip so nothing decays: 10 minutes a day from the 2-hour maintenance pot.
Step 5 — in the last 5 days, switch to timed mocks so speed is trained, not just accuracy.
**Final answer:** 10 h listening (transcript method), 4 h grammar, 4 h reading (timed passages), 2 h vocabulary maintenance; final 5 days = timed mocks with an error log.

⚠️ **Common trap:** revising the skill you are already good at, because it feels productive. Reading at 70% is comfortable; listening at 40% is where the marks are. Allocate hours by weakness, and check the sectional minimum rule before you decide that a high total is enough.

## Solved examples

**Example 1 (easy).** Read and answer: この みせは 日曜日に あいて いますか。 Notice: `営業時間 午前十時〜午後八時　定休日 日曜日・祝日`.

Step 1 — あいて います (aite imasu) = "is open" (from あく, to open). Step 2 — hunt for 日曜日: it appears after 定休日. Step 3 — 定休日 = regular closing day, so Sunday is closed. Step 4 — 祝日 (shukujitsu) = public holiday, also closed — extra information, not needed for this question.
**Answer:** いいえ、あいて いません。日曜日は 定休日です。

**Example 2 (medium).** Read the passage line and answer: マリさんは なにを しますか。
`マリさんは あした ともだちと あいます。それから、えいがを 見ます。`

Step 1 — question asks **what** she does, so hunt for a verb with を. Step 2 — two actions appear: ともだちと あいます (meets a friend) and えいがを 見ます (watches a film). Step 3 — それから marks the second as the later action, and the question asks about the plan, so both are part of the answer. Step 4 — あした is a relative time word, so no に — do not add one when you rewrite.
**Answer:** ともだちと あって、それから えいがを 見ます。

**Example 3 (medium-hard).** You hear: `女：あしたの パーティーは 七時からです。でも、六時半までに 来て ください。` Question: 何時に パーティーが はじまりますか。 (What time does the party start?)

Step 1 — fix the question: はじまります (hajimarimasu) = begins, so the answer is the **start time**, not the arrival deadline. Step 2 — 七時から = starts at seven. Step 3 — 六時半までに 来て ください is an instruction about arriving, a decoy for this question. Step 4 — do not confuse までに (by) with から (from).
**Answer:** 七時に はじまります。

**Example 4 (exam level).** Write a four-sentence diary entry about today: it rained in the morning, you studied Japanese in the afternoon, the test was difficult, and you were tired.

Step 1 — date line: 七月十日. Step 2 — sentence 1, past noun sentence: あさは あめでした。 Step 3 — sentence 2, past verb: ごご、にほんごを べんきょうしました。 Step 4 — sentence 3, い-adjective past: テストは むずかしかったです。 Step 5 — sentence 4, い-adjective past for feeling: とても つかれました。 (つかれる is a verb, so ました, not かったです.) Step 6 — check every ending is past, and every sentence ends with 。
**Answer:**
七月十日
あさは あめでした。ごご、にほんごを べんきょうしました。テストは むずかしかったです。とても つかれました。

## Common traps and mistakes

- **Translating sign words literally.** 立入禁止 contains 入 (enter) but forbids entry; 非常口 is an emergency exit, not a normal one. Cost: the notice questions.
- **Answering a passage question from general knowledge.** The text decides, not real life.
- **Missing the negative at the end of a listening sentence.** 行きます vs 行きません differ in the last syllable; stopping early flips the answer.
- **Taking the first number you hear.** N5 scripts correct themselves mid-sentence with でも / あ、すみません.
- **Confusing から (start) with までに (deadline).** A classic 課題理解 decoy pair.
- **Reading ちょっと… / けっこうです / だいじょうぶです as agreement.** They are polite refusals.
- **One-word answers in speaking.** はい、カレー。 loses the sentence-formation marks; カレーが すきです。 earns them.
- **Using おとうさん / おかあさん for your own family.** Use ちち / はは when speaking about your own parents.
- **Mixing tenses in a diary.** The date fixes the tense; finished events take ました/でした/かったです.
- **い-adjective + でした.** おもしろいでした and きれいかったです are both wrong: い → かったです; な/noun → でした.
- **Writing particles in kanji, or dropping okurigana.** 食ます for 食べます loses marks for spelling.

## Quick revision box

- ✅ Reading: read the question first, hunt katakana/numbers, then find the one answer sentence and check its polarity.
- ✅ Signs are blocks, not grammar: 入口 / 出口 / 立入禁止 / 営業時間 / 定休日 / 非常口 / 無料.
- ✅ Listening: the question comes first, hold it while decoys pass, and the deciding verb is at the end — the four N5 types are 課題理解, ポイント理解, 発話表現, 即時応答.
- ✅ Numbers that get tested: よじ, しちじ, くじ, いっぷん, ろっぷん, はっぷん, じゅっぷん, ろっぴゃく, はっぴゃく.
- ✅ Speaking: answer = direct answer + one detail + one extra sentence; stay in です/ます.
- ✅ Self-intro order: greeting → name (～と いいます) → origin → status → hobby → family → よろしく おねがいします.
- ✅ Writing: 。 and 、, no spaces, particles in kana, い→かったです / な→でした, consistent tense.
- ✅ Revision is by weakness, not by comfort — the sectional minimum means a weak skill can fail you alone.

## Practice questions

1. Match each sign to its meaning: (a) 立入禁止 (b) 定休日 (c) 非常口 (d) 無料 — meanings: 1 free of charge, 2 no entry, 3 emergency exit, 4 regular closing day.

2. Read and answer. `わたしの がっこうは えきから ちかいです。あるいて 十分です。がっこうの となりに としょかんが あります。としょかんは 日曜日に やすみです。`
   (a) がっこうまで あるいて なんぷんですか。 (b) 日曜日に としょかんで べんきょうする ことが できますか。

3. You hear: `男：コーヒーを のみませんか。女：コーヒーは ちょっと…。こうちゃが いいです。` Question: 女の人は 何を のみますか。 Explain the trap.

4. Write a 4-line message to 田中さん: the party is at seven, meet in front of the station, please reply. Add your name at the end.

5. Fix the errors: `きのうは あついでした。わたしは こうえんへ 行きます。とても きれいかったです。`

6. You have one week left and your listening score is your weakest. Give a 3-point plan, and say why passive listening alone will not help.

## Answers

1. (a) **2 no entry** — 立入禁止 (たちいりきんし) forbids entry despite containing 入. (b) **4 regular closing day** — 定休日 (ていきゅうび). (c) **3 emergency exit** — 非常口 (ひじょうぐち). (d) **1 free of charge** — 無料 (むりょう).

2. (a) **十分です** — the passage says あるいて 十分 (ten minutes on foot); note 十分 is じゅっぷん. (b) **いいえ、できません** — としょかんは 日曜日に やすみです, so it is closed on Sundays.

3. **こうちゃを のみます。** The trap is ちょっと…, which is a polite refusal, not hesitation — she is declining coffee. Answering コーヒー means you ignored the softener. Key step: ちょっと… = no; が いいです = the actual choice.

4. Model answer:
   田中さん
   あしたの パーティーは 七時からです。えきの まえで あいましょう。
   へんじを ください。　　ラジャ
   Check: addressee first, time given with から, proposal with あいましょう, request with ください, name last.

5. **きのうは あつかったです。わたしは こうえんへ 行きました。とても きれいでした。** Three fixes: あついでした → あつかったです (い-adjective past); 行きます → 行きました (きのう makes it past); きれいかったです → きれいでした (きれい is a な-adjective).

6. Plan: (i) 15 minutes daily with the transcript — blind listen, read transcript, listen again; (ii) drill the number/time readings aloud (よじ, しちじ, くじ, ろっぷん, はっぴゃく); (iii) one timed mock listening paper, then log every error by cause (word / number / negation / speed). Passive listening alone fails because without the transcript you cannot tell which word you missed, so the same error repeats; the transcript pass is what converts "I heard noise" into "I missed までに".
