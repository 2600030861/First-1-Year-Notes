## Course Overview

This course develops the **English language, communication, professional writing, speaking, and workplace etiquette skills** required by engineers and other professionals.

The course focuses on:

* Accuracy and clarity in English
* Professional vocabulary
* Technical and professional writing
* Corporate email and correspondence
* Spoken English and professional conversations
* Corporate etiquette and professional presence
* Responsible use of AI in communication

# CO1 – Module 1: Foundations of English for the Engineer

## Accuracy, Clarity & Register

### 1. Importance of Language Accuracy

* Why grammatical and language accuracy matters in professional communication
* Impact of errors on:

  * Professional credibility
  * Clarity
  * Understanding
  * Workplace relationships
  * Technical communication

### 2. Sentence Fundamentals

* Parts of a sentence
* Subject and predicate
* Subject–verb agreement
* Sentence structure
* Simple, compound, and complex sentences
* Fragments and run-on sentences
* Correct word order

### 3. Common Errors in Indian/Engineering English

* Subject–verb agreement errors
* Incorrect articles
* Incorrect prepositions
* Wrong tense usage
* Incorrect word order
* Redundancy
* Direct translation from regional languages
* Misuse of commonly confused words
* Incorrect use of singular/plural forms

### 4. Punctuation for Clarity

* Full stop
* Comma
* Semicolon
* Colon
* Apostrophe
* Quotation marks
* Question mark
* Exclamation mark
* Hyphen and dash
* How punctuation changes meaning

### 5. Principles of Clarity

Effective professional writing should be:

* Clear
* Direct
* Specific
* Logical
* Easy to understand

Avoid:

* Ambiguous statements
* Unnecessary complexity
* Excessive jargon
* Long and confusing sentences

### 6. Register and Tone

* Meaning of register
* Formal and informal language
* Professional register
* Academic register
* Appropriate tone for different audiences and situations
* Choosing language according to context

### 7. Concision

* Expressing ideas using fewer but meaningful words
* Removing unnecessary words
* Avoiding repetition
* Avoiding redundant expressions

### 8. Standard Varieties and Intelligibility

* Standard English varieties
* Global English
* Indian English
* Importance of intelligibility
* Understanding different accents and varieties

### 9. Proofreading and Self-Editing

* Reading carefully for errors
* Checking grammar and punctuation

# CO2 – Module 2: Vocabulary & Word Precision

## 1. Building Professional Vocabulary

* Developing vocabulary systematically
* Learning words through context
* Understanding word families
* Learning synonyms and antonyms
* Using dictionaries and reliable language resources

## 2. Precision in Word Choice

* Selecting the exact word for the intended meaning
* Avoiding vague words
* Understanding differences between similar words
* Considering context before choosing a word

### Example:

Instead of:

> The machine has a problem.

More precise:

> The machine is experiencing an overheating issue.

## 3. Professional and Corporate Vocabulary

* Workplace terminology
* Business vocabulary
* Professional expressions
* Vocabulary used in meetings, reports, projects, and organizations

## 4. Technical vs General Vocabulary

* Technical terms used within a specific field
* General vocabulary understood by a wider audience
* Knowing when technical terminology is appropriate
* Avoiding unnecessary jargon

## 5. Explaining Technical Ideas to Non-Technical Audiences

An engineer should be able to:

* Simplify complex concepts
* Avoid unnecessary technical terminology
* Define unavoidable technical terms
* Use examples and analogies
* Focus on the audience's level of knowledge

## 6. Collocations and Natural Usage

* Words that naturally occur together
* Correct combinations of words
* Developing natural professional English

### Examples:

* Make a decision
* Meet a deadline
* Conduct research
* Solve a problem
* Raise a concern
* Take responsibility

## 7. Idioms, Phrasal Verbs & Professional Expressions

* Common idioms
* Common phrasal verbs
* Professional expressions
* Appropriate use according to context

### Important:

Misusing idioms or informal expressions can make professional communication unclear or inappropriate.

## 8. Tone-Carrying Words

Some words communicate additional attitudes or emotions.

Learn to identify words that sound:

* Positive
* Negative
* Neutral
* Polite
* Aggressive
* Formal
* Informal

## 9. Avoiding Clichés and Buzzwords

Avoid:

* Overused clichés
* Empty buzzwords
* Unnecessary corporate language
* Vague expressions

### Example:

Weak:

> We need to leverage our core competencies to optimize synergies.

Better:

> We need to use our existing skills to improve the project.

### Key Principle:

**Write with substance rather than sounding unnecessarily corporate.**

## 10. AI for Vocabulary Growth

AI can be used to:

* Learn new words
* Generate contextual examples
* Compare synonyms
* Understand word differences
* Practice professional vocabulary
* Improve word choice

# CO3 – Module 3: Professional Writing & Drafting

## 1. Qualities of Effective Professional Writing

Professional writing should be:

* Accurate
* Clear
* Concise
* Coherent
* Relevant
* Well-structured
* Audience-focused
* Professional

## 2. Audience and Purpose Analysis

Before writing, identify:

### Audience

* Who will read the document?
* What do they already know?
* What information do they need?

### Purpose

* To inform
* To request
* To explain
* To persuade
* To instruct
* To report

## 3. Structuring Writing

A well-structured document generally contains:

1. Introduction
2. Main discussion
3. Supporting information
4. Conclusion/action

Use:

* Headings
* Subheadings
* Paragraphs
* Lists
* Logical ordering

## 4. Coherence and Cohesion

### Coherence

The ideas make logical sense as a whole.

### Cohesion

The sentences and paragraphs are connected using:

* Linking words
* Pronouns
* Repetition of key terms
* Transitions
* Logical relationships

### Common Linking Words

* Therefore
* However
* Furthermore
* In addition
* Consequently
* For example
* In contrast

## 5. Common Short Professional Documents

Examples include:

* Notices
* Memos
* Short reports
* Proposals
* Instructions
* Summaries
* Professional messages
* Applications
* Requests

## 6. Plain-Language Writing and Readability

Plain language means communicating information in a way that the intended audience can easily understand.

Use:

* Familiar words
* Shorter sentences
* Active voice where appropriate
* Clear headings
* Direct statements

Avoid:

* Unnecessary jargon
* Complex sentence structures
* Unnecessary technical language

## 7. The Writing Process

### Step 1 – Planning

Identify:

* Purpose
* Audience
* Main ideas

### Step 2 – Drafting

Write the first version.

### Step 3 – Revising

Improve:

* Organization
* Clarity
* Content
* Logic

### Step 4 – Editing

Correct:

* Grammar
* Vocabulary
* Sentence structure
* Style

### Step 5 – Proofreading

Check for final:

* Spelling
* Punctuation
* Formatting
* Typographical errors

## 8. Summarising and Paraphrasing

### Summarising

Expressing the main ideas of a longer text in a shorter form.

### Paraphrasing

Expressing someone else's idea in your own words while maintaining the original meaning.

### Important Rules

* Do not distort the original meaning.
* Do not copy sentence structures unnecessarily.
* Give credit to the original source where required.
* Avoid plagiarism.

## 9. Quoting and Attribution

* Use quotation marks for direct quotations.
* Identify the original source.
* Distinguish your ideas from another person's ideas.
* Attribute information appropriately.

## 10. Tone in Writing

Tone should match:

* Audience
* Purpose
* Relationship
* Context

Possible tones:

* Formal
* Neutral
* Friendly
* Respectful
* Persuasive
* Apologetic
* Assertive

## 11. AI as a Drafting and Revision Partner

AI can help with:

* Brainstorming
* Creating first drafts
* Improving structure
* Grammar checking
* Rewriting for clarity
* Adjusting tone

However:

* Verify AI-generated information.
* Maintain your own judgment.
* Do not submit AI-generated work blindly.
* Check for inaccurate or fabricated information.

# CO4 – Module 4: Corporate Email & Written Correspondence

## 1. Anatomy of a Professional Email

A professional email generally contains:

1. Recipient
2. Subject line
3. Greeting
4. Opening/context
5. Main message
6. Required action
7. Closing
8. Signature

## 2. Effective Subject Lines

A good subject line should be:

* Specific
* Short
* Relevant
* Action-oriented when appropriate

### Example:

Weak:

> Important

Better:

> Request for Project Meeting on Monday

## 3. Email for Different Purposes

Professional emails may be written to:

* Make a request
* Provide information
* Ask for clarification
* Submit documents
* Make an enquiry
* Follow up
* Confirm information
* Apologize
* Give instructions
* Report an issue

Each purpose requires an appropriate structure and tone.

## 4. Tone and Register in Email

Use:

* Professional language
* Respectful expressions
* Appropriate formality
* Clear requests

Avoid:

* Aggressive language
* Excessive informality
* Slang
* Unnecessary emotional language

## 5. Email Etiquette

Important practices:

* Use meaningful subject lines.
* Check recipients carefully.
* Use CC and BCC appropriately.
* Avoid unnecessary "Reply All."
* Proofread before sending.
* Respond within a reasonable time.
* Avoid writing emails entirely in CAPITAL LETTERS.
* Attach the correct files.
* Mention attachments in the message.

## 6. Clarity and Brevity

A professional email should:

* State the purpose early
* Use short paragraphs
* Focus on relevant information
* Clearly identify required action
* Avoid unnecessary details

## 7. Handling Difficult Messages

When communicating difficult information:

* Remain professional
* State facts clearly
* Avoid personal attacks
* Explain the situation
* Suggest solutions where appropriate
* Maintain respectful language

## 8. Cross-Cultural and Global Email

Consider:

* Different communication styles
* Time zones
* Holidays
* Cultural expectations
* Levels of formality
* Different interpretations of tone

Avoid assumptions about how people from another culture communicate.

## 9. Other Written Correspondence

### Formal Letters

* Official requests
* Applications
* Complaints
* Enquiries
* Responses

### Official Requests

Should clearly state:

* What is being requested
* Why it is needed
* Relevant details
* Expected action

### Professional Messaging Apps

* Maintain professional tone
* Avoid excessive emojis
* Avoid unnecessary messages
* Respect working hours
* Keep messages concise
* Do not assume instant replies

## 10. AI for Correspondence

AI can assist with:

* Drafting emails
* Improving tone
* Making messages concise
* Checking grammar
* Creating formal versions

Always review the final message before sending it.

# CO5 – Module 5: Spoken English & Professional Conversation

## 1. Spoken vs Written English

### Spoken English

Usually:

* More immediate
* More interactive
* More dependent on context
* Includes pauses and conversational expressions

### Written English

Usually:

* More planned
* More structured
* More permanent
* Requires greater attention to grammar and punctuation

## 2. Intelligibility Over Accent

The primary goal of professional speech is:

> **Being clearly understood.**

Focus on:

* Pronunciation
* Word stress
* Sentence stress
* Pace
* Volume
* Clear articulation

Having an accent is not itself a communication problem.

## 3. Professional Introduction

A professional self-introduction may include:

* Name
* Educational/professional background
* Skills
* Area of interest
* Current role
* Relevant goals

Keep it:

* Brief
* Relevant
* Confident
* Professional

## 4. Conversation Skills

Effective professional conversation requires:

* Confidence
* Politeness
* Turn-taking
* Appropriate body language
* Clear speech
* Respect for others

## 5. Active Listening

Active listening involves:

* Paying attention
* Avoiding unnecessary interruptions
* Understanding the speaker
* Asking relevant questions
* Confirming understanding
* Responding appropriately

## 6. Asking and Answering Professionally

### Asking

Use polite and clear questions.

### Answering

* Listen carefully
* Answer directly
* Provide relevant details
* Ask for clarification if necessary

## 7. Expressing Opinions

Useful professional expressions include:

* In my opinion...
* I believe that...
* From my perspective...
* I would suggest...
* Based on the available information...

## 8. Agreeing and Disagreeing Politely

### Agreeing

* I agree with your point.
* That is a good observation.
* I think that is reasonable.

### Disagreeing

Disagreement should focus on the **idea**, not the person.

Use:

* I see your point, but...
* I have a slightly different view.
* I understand the concern; however...

## 9. Suggestions, Requests and Refusals

### Suggesting

* We could consider...
* I would suggest...
* Perhaps we could...

### Requesting

* Could you please...?
* Would you mind...?
* Could I ask you to...?

### Refusing Politely

* I'm afraid I won't be able to...
* Unfortunately, I cannot...
* I appreciate the request, but...

## 10. Telephone and Video-Call Etiquette

### Telephone

* Introduce yourself
* Speak clearly
* Confirm important information
* Listen carefully
* End professionally

### Video Calls

* Join on time
* Check microphone and camera
* Maintain professional appearance
* Avoid unnecessary background noise
* Stay attentive
* Mute when appropriate

## 11. Everyday Work Situations

Professional spoken English is used in:

* Meetings
* Interviews
* Presentations
* Discussions
* Teamwork
* Customer interactions
* Problem-solving
* Workplace conversations

## 12. AI as a Spoken-English Partner

AI can help with:

* Conversation practice
* Interview practice
* Pronunciation practice
* Vocabulary practice
* Role-play situations
* Feedback on communication

# CO6 – Module 6: Corporate Etiquette & Professional Presence

## 1. Meaning of Professional Etiquette

Professional etiquette refers to the accepted standards of **respectful, appropriate, and professional behaviour** in the workplace.

It communicates:

* Competence
* Respect
* Reliability
* Professionalism

## 2. First Impressions and Credibility

First impressions can be influenced by:

* Communication
* Appearance
* Punctuality
* Body language
* Confidence
* Courtesy
* Preparedness

Professional credibility is built over time through consistent behaviour.

## 3. Workplace Communication Norms

Important norms include:

* Respectful communication
* Appropriate language
* Active listening
* Timely responses
* Clear communication
* Respect for workplace hierarchy and roles

## 4. Meeting Etiquette

Before a meeting:

* Prepare necessary information.
* Read the agenda.
* Join on time.

During a meeting:

* Listen actively.
* Avoid unnecessary interruptions.
* Stay on topic.
* Respect different opinions.
* Contribute constructively.

After a meeting:

* Follow assigned actions.
* Meet deadlines.
* Share required information.

## 5. Channel Etiquette

Different communication channels have different conventions.

### Email

Formal and structured.

### Messaging Apps

Short and direct.

### Phone

Immediate and conversational.

### Video Meetings

Interactive and visual.

Choose the channel appropriate to:

* Urgency
* Formality
* Complexity
* Confidentiality

## 6. Cross-Cultural Communication

Effective global communication requires:

* Cultural awareness
* Respect
* Flexibility
* Avoiding stereotypes
* Awareness of different communication styles

## 7. Digital Professionalism and Netiquette

### Good Digital Practices

* Use professional language.
* Proofread messages.
* Protect confidential information.
* Respect others online.
* Use appropriate profile information.
* Avoid offensive content.
* Respond appropriately.

### Netiquette

**Netiquette = Network + Etiquette**

It refers to appropriate behaviour when communicating online.

## 8. Handling Difficult Interactions

When facing difficult situations:

1. Remain calm.
2. Listen carefully.
3. Avoid personal attacks.
4. Focus on the issue.
5. Communicate respectfully.
6. Look for a practical solution.
7. Escalate appropriately when necessary.

## 9. Inclusive and Respectful Language

Use language that:

* Respects individuals
* Avoids unnecessary stereotypes
* Does not discriminate
* Recognizes diversity
* Focuses on people respectfully

Avoid language that is:

* Insulting
* Discriminatory
* Stereotyping
* Unnecessarily exclusionary

## 10. Professional Presence

Professional presence is the way a person communicates competence, confidence, reliability, and professionalism.

It involves:

* Communication skills
* Confidence
* Listening
* Body language
* Professional behaviour
* Digital presence
* Reliability

## 11. Personal Brand in Communication

A professional personal brand is built through consistent:

* Communication
* Behaviour
* Expertise
* Values
* Online presence
* Professional relationships

### Key Principle:

**Your communication contributes to how others perceive your professional identity.**

## 12. Responsible Use of AI in Professional Communication

AI should be used responsibly.

### Appropriate Uses

* Grammar correction
* Brainstorming
* Draft improvement
* Vocabulary practice
* Tone adjustment
* Communication practice

### Responsibilities

* Verify information.
* Protect confidential information.
* Avoid sharing sensitive workplace data unnecessarily.
* Review AI-generated content.
* Maintain human judgment.
* Do not misrepresent AI-generated work when disclosure is required.
